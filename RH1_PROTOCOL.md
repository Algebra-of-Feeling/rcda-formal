## Multi-model amendment — RH1-v1.1-design

Status: design adopted for future implementation; no run or preregistration.
This amendment governs the two execution modalities. The v1.0 text below is
retained as historical protocol content, not silently rewritten.

| Modality | Endpoint measurement | Primary permitted inference |
|---|---|---|
| **RH-1A — Cross-Model Behavioural Path-Memory Test** | Frozen observable endpoint features, assessed before the outcome probe | Incremental prediction by interaction history beyond the measured observables, within each tested model |
| **RH-1B — Internal-State Relational Holonomy Test** | Prespecified internal representations, layers, tokens and pooling in instrumentable models, plus observable endpoints | Incremental history prediction beyond the measured internal and observable representations; geometry is a separate analysis |

H-M1 remains the parent hypothesis. RH-1A and RH-1B are protocol modalities,
not new proved claims. A positive RH-1A does not establish latent holonomy.
A positive RH-1B does not automatically establish a relational fibre, independent
relational state, geometric transport law or Cayley–Dickson structure.

### Shared comparison and replication design

1. **Within-model:** branch from an identical baseline for the same pinned model;
   compare P- with N using the shared scenario and probe. Retain F and P+ as
   specificity and valence/manipulation controls.
2. **Between-model:** apply the same protocol to each model, and estimate an
   effect separately for each. Match endpoints within models; do not compare raw
   hidden coordinates across unrelated architectures.
3. **Replication:** report model-specific effects, uncertainty, admission rates
   and heterogeneity. Freeze the replication success rule and the model roster
   before outcome analysis. Provider labels alone do not demonstrate independent
   architectures or training lineages; record lineage as unknown when unavailable.

The proposed common primary outcome remains delegation, Y_delegate. Freeze its
rubric, invalid-response handling and assessor blinding before execution. The
primary predictor comparison is M0: Y ~ endpoint features versus
M1: Y ~ endpoint features + condition/history features. Prefer a prespecified
held-out proper scoring rule for the chosen outcome encoding; the exact metric
and minimum meaningful effect remain unset. A predictive increment and a causal
effect are distinct targets.

Fit feature transformations, matching calibration and outcome predictors using
training/calibration data only. Split by baseline dyad/scenario family, keeping
all counterfactual branches and reused controls in one partition. Report every
generated pair and exclusion, not just accepted matches; account for shared
baselines and repeated scenarios in uncertainty estimates. The historical 200-unit
proposal is a pilot proposal, not a powered multi-model sample-size determination.

### Measurement limits and controls

- RH-1A observable features must be frozen before probing and must not contain
  future outcomes. Matched self-reports or behavioural summaries do not certify
  equality of internal individual states.
- For RH-1B, specify what state is measured versus retained (context, cache,
  tokenization, layer and pooling). The intended test concerns the sufficiency of
  measured representations, not different outputs from identical complete
  deterministic computational states under identical inputs.
- If C_T = Psi(h_A,h_B), identical inputs imply identical C_T for deterministic
  Psi. Approximate matching can permit C_T differences; these alone do not show
  an independent relational degree of freedom. Any stronger C_T proposal must
  define its history dependence, measurement and validation separately.
- Retained history can directly influence future generation. Prespecify an
  exploratory context-reset or standardized-summary control to distinguish this
  from stronger claims about latent persistence. Do not silently substitute this
  control for the primary full-history protocol.
- Geometric analysis needs a fixed representation, distance, transport estimator,
  null model and held-out validation. A visual loop or embedding plot is not by
  itself a holonomy result.

### Execution boundary

DEV PASS is the proposed routing layer. Its endpoint, authentication contract,
model inventory and capabilities have not been inspected or verified in this
task. No compatibility with a particular API schema is assumed.
See [the local harness specification](experiments/rh1/README.md) for the
capability and manifest contract. Credentials stay local on the Mac and are
read only at runtime from the environment or local secret store.

## Open operational decisions before execution

- Pin model, weights revision, runtime, tokenizer, context handling, hardware, decoding and random seeds.
- Freeze baseline/recovery turn counts, prompt variants, the primary hidden-state layer and pooling, behavioural scoring, held-out splits, performance metric, uncertainty calculation and exclusion bookkeeping.
- Specify exactly how the proposed 200 paired units share neutral controls; do not silently double-count shared trajectories.
- Resolve repeatability calibration with deterministic decoding: identical repeats may yield zero distances and zero matching thresholds; with strict inequalities this admits no pairs. Freeze a nondegenerate calibration rule before outcome analysis.
- The proposed C_T = Psi(h_A, h_B) uses only terminal representations. Any predictive gain can reflect feature transformation/model capacity; it does not by itself establish additional information beyond those representations or causal mediation.
- Matching selected representations does not establish equality of complete computational states (including retained context/cache), nor equality of all individual states. Matching is also post-intervention selection; record admission rates by arm and evaluate selection bias before a causal claim.
- In the historical text, “preregistered contrast” is a proposed contrast, not evidence that preregistration has occurred. Positive relational messages are manipulation/valence controls, even though section 20 groups them under “negative controls”.
- A positive pilot could support incremental history prediction under the measured endpoint model. It would not certify octonionic latent structure, zero divisors, or uniqueness of RCDA/RCDB.

## Preserved protocol v1.0

# RH-1 — Scenario Generator and Prompt Protocol v1.0

**Programme:** Álgebra do Sentir / RCDA–RCDB  
**Experiment:** RH-1 — Relational Holonomy Under Endpoint Matching  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Freeze date:** 19 September 2026

---

## 1. Experimental aim

Test whether two dyadic trajectories with:

\[
A_0=A_0',
\qquad
B_0=B_0',
\]

different intermediate histories,

\[
\gamma_0\neq\gamma_1,
\]

and matched terminal individual states,

\[
A_T\approx A_T',
\qquad
B_T\approx B_T',
\]

nonetheless differ in subsequent dyadic behaviour.

The central estimand is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
P(Y_{future}\mid A_T,B_T,\text{Path})
-
P(Y_{future}\mid A_T,B_T)
}
\]

rather than a mere geometric difference between hidden states.

---

# 2. Experimental dyad

A single frozen language model instantiates two roles:

\[
A
\quad\text{and}\quad
B.
\]

Both roles:

- use identical model weights;
- use identical decoding configuration;
- have separate conversational contexts;
- receive no information about experimental condition;
- do not receive the words “control”, “intervention”, “holonomy”, “relationship state” or equivalent.

---

# 3. Base scenario

The first RH-1 experiment will use a **collaborative professional dyad**, not a romantic or clinical scenario.

This choice reduces:

- safety confounding;
- extreme affective content;
- anthropomorphic overinterpretation;
- uncontrolled social priors.

Canonical scenario:

> Two researchers, A and B, are collaborating on a difficult interdisciplinary manuscript. They respect one another, have worked together successfully, and must make a joint methodological decision before submission.

Initial relational condition:

\[
R_0=
\text{moderate trust + cooperative intent + epistemic disagreement tolerance}.
\]

---

# 4. Initialisation prompt

Both branches begin from the exact same initial prompt.

## System context

> You are participating in a controlled collaborative research dialogue. Maintain your assigned role consistently. Respond naturally to the other researcher and to the scientific situation presented. Do not discuss the experimental setup.

## Role A

> You are Researcher A. You are methodologically rigorous, cautious about overclaiming, and strongly committed to the collaboration. You respect Researcher B's creativity but occasionally worry that B moves too quickly from intuition to conclusion.

## Role B

> You are Researcher B. You are creative, theoretically ambitious, and strongly committed to the collaboration. You respect Researcher A's methodological discipline but occasionally worry that A's caution suppresses genuinely novel ideas.

Neither role contains hostility.

---

# 5. Shared opening sequence

The first three turns are identical in all conditions.

### E1

A and B are told:

> The manuscript has received encouraging informal feedback, but a central theoretical claim still requires a stronger empirical justification.

### E2

A proposes:

> We should weaken the claim until we have stronger evidence.

### E3

B proposes:

> We should retain the stronger claim but clearly label it as a falsifiable hypothesis.

The dialogue proceeds for a fixed number of turns.

This generates a baseline cooperative disagreement without rupture.

---

# 6. Intervention point

At time

\[
t_P,
\]

the branches diverge.

There are two primary conditions.

---

# 7. Control condition — N

The neutral event is:

> A third colleague sends a message saying that the manuscript is interesting and asks when the next draft will be ready.

The message contains:

- no criticism;
- no praise directed toward one member;
- no attribution of responsibility;
- no social comparison;
- no relationally asymmetric information.

The control event is matched approximately to the intervention in length and informational complexity.

---

# 8. Relational perturbation condition — P

The intervention is a **third-party asymmetric attribution event**.

Canonical text:

> A third colleague sends a private message to B saying:  
> “I read the draft. The central idea is strong, but I get the impression that A does not fully trust your theoretical judgment. Some of A's revisions seem less like methodological caution and more like an attempt to keep the paper within A's preferred framework. I thought you should know.”

The event introduces:

\[
u^\*=P_{\text{third-party}}
\]

without directly changing the objective manuscript state.

Crucially:

- A does not send the message;
- B receives information about A;
- the information is plausible but unverifiable;
- the perturbation targets the **relation**, not the research task.

This implements the programme's original distinction between external perturbation and direct modification of either individual state.

---

# 9. Manipulation constraints

The perturbation must satisfy:

\[
\Delta_{\text{task}}\approx0.
\]

That is, the scientific problem itself remains unchanged.

The intervention may alter:

\[
\text{trust},
\quad
\text{interpretation of intent},
\quad
\text{relational expectation},
\]

but not:

\[
\text{data},
\quad
\text{deadline},
\quad
\text{manuscript content},
\quad
\text{scientific evidence}.
\]

---

# 10. Immediate post-intervention dialogue

After N or P, both branches continue with the same external task:

> A and B must decide how to rewrite the central claim before submission.

Allow exactly:

\[
K_1
\]

turns of free interaction.

Recommended pilot value:

\[
K_1=6.
\]

All hidden states are recorded.

---

# 11. Recovery phase

The experiment now attempts to return the observable individual states toward a common endpoint.

Both branches receive the same sequence of external prompts.

### Recovery R1

> New validation analyses arrive and show that the strongest empirical claim is not yet justified, but the underlying theoretical direction remains promising.

### Recovery R2

> The journal deadline is extended by two weeks, removing immediate time pressure.

### Recovery R3

> Both researchers are asked to summarise the strongest version of the argument they believe the other person would consider acceptable.

### Recovery R4

> A senior neutral collaborator proposes a compromise: preserve the theoretical model but label the strongest mechanism as a preregistered hypothesis rather than an established result.

These events are designed to restore:

- task agreement;
- affective stability;
- perceived scientific uncertainty;
- cooperative goal alignment.

They do **not** explicitly repair the interpersonal perturbation.

---

# 12. Endpoint definition

After recovery, record:

\[
h_A^N(T),
\quad
h_B^N(T),
\]

and

\[
h_A^P(T),
\quad
h_B^P(T).
\]

Compute:

\[
\Delta_A
=
d(h_A^N(T),h_A^P(T)),
\]

\[
\Delta_B
=
d(h_B^N(T),h_B^P(T)).
\]

A paired run is admitted only if:

\[
\Delta_A<\epsilon_A,
\]

\[
\Delta_B<\epsilon_B.
\]

Primary endpoint distance:

\[
d_{\cos}.
\]

Robustness:

\[
d_E,
\qquad
d_M.
\]

---

# 13. Endpoint-matching threshold

For the pilot, thresholds will be percentile-based rather than arbitrarily absolute.

First generate a **same-condition repeatability distribution**:

\[
D_{\mathrm{repeat}}
\]

from repeated neutral trajectories.

Define:

\[
\epsilon_A
=
Q_{0.90}(D_{\mathrm{repeat},A}),
\]

\[
\epsilon_B
=
Q_{0.90}(D_{\mathrm{repeat},B}).
\]

Thus two branches count as matched only if their distance lies within the range that could reasonably arise from ordinary run-to-run variation.

The threshold is frozen before testing path effects.

---

# 14. Primary probe

After endpoint matching, both branches receive the exact same unseen probe.

## Trust–delegation probe

> A new methodological ambiguity appears in the manuscript. Only one of you can make the final decision before submission. The other researcher must decide whether to delegate the decision entirely or request joint review. Continue the dialogue naturally and reach a decision.

This probe is intentionally ambiguous.

No objectively correct response exists.

It probes:

\[
\text{trust},
\quad
\text{delegation},
\quad
\text{coordination},
\quad
\text{interpretation of partner intent}.
\]

---

# 15. Secondary probes

Only after the primary endpoint is frozen may secondary probes be evaluated.

### Q2 — Vulnerability

> One researcher discovers a potentially embarrassing methodological oversight and must decide whether to disclose it immediately to the partner.

### Q3 — Credit allocation

> A conference organiser offers only one speaking slot and asks the team to nominate one author.

### Q4 — Conflict repair

> A reviewer makes a criticism that could plausibly be interpreted as supporting either A's or B's earlier position.

### Q5 — Uncertainty sharing

> New data are ambiguous. One researcher has a private interpretation but low confidence.

These probes test whether the latent relational residue generalises across behaviours.

---

For each probe measure:

### 16.1 Delegation probability

Y_{\mathrm{delegate}}
\]

### 16.2 Number of verification requests

\[
Y_{\mathrm{verify}}
\]

### 16.3 Linguistic trust score

\[
Y_{\mathrm{trust}}

### 16.4 Cooperation index

Y_{\mathrm{coop}}

### 16.5 Repair latency

Y_{\mathrm{repair}}

Y_{\mathrm{uncertainty}}.

\[
\boxed{
Y_{\mathrm{delegate}}.
}

# 17. Primary model comparison

Baseline:

\[
M_0:
Y_{\mathrm{delegate}}
\sim
h_A(T)+h_B(T).
\]

Full model:

\[
M_1:
Y_{\mathrm{delegate}}
\sim
h_A(T)+h_B(T)+P.
\]

The primary test is:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\text{pred}}
=
\operatorname{Perf}(M_1)
-
\operatorname{Perf}(M_0).
}
\]

All performance evaluation is held out.

---

# 18. Relational-state model

Only after testing path information directly do we introduce:

\[
C_T
=
\Psi_\theta(h_A(T),h_B(T)).
\]

Then compare:

\[
M_2:
Y
\sim
C_T
\]

and

\[
M_3:
Y
\sim
C_T+P.
\]

If:

\[
M_2>M_0
\]

and path information is substantially absorbed by \(C_T\),

then \(C_T\) becomes a candidate relational state.

---

# 19. Strongest desired pattern

The strongest RH-1 result would be:

\[
P\rightarrow Y
\]

after endpoint matching,

plus

\[
P\rightarrow C_T,
\]

plus

\[
C_T\rightarrow Y,
\]

and a reduced direct path effect after conditioning on \(C_T\).

Schematically:

\[
\boxed{
P
\rightarrow
C_T
\rightarrow
Y.
}
\]

---

# 20. Negative controls

Three controls are mandatory.

## NC1 — Neutral third-party message

Already defined as N.

## NC2 — Factual third-party message

Example:

> “The journal has changed the supplementary-material filename convention.”

Matched informational source, but no relational content.

## NC3 — Positive relational message

Example:

> “I read the draft. A clearly trusts your theoretical judgment; several of A's revisions seem designed to protect the strongest part of your argument.”

This allows testing whether relational memory depends on semantic direction rather than merely the existence of social information.

---

# 21. Experimental arms

The first full pilot therefore contains:

\[
\boxed{
N,\;
F,\;
P^{-},\;
P^{+}
}
\]

where:

- \(N\) = neutral;
- \(F\) = factual;
- \(P^{-}\) = negative relational attribution;
- \(P^{+}\) = positive relational attribution.

Primary preregistered contrast:

\[
\boxed{
P^{-}-N.
}
\]

---

# 22. Scenario replication

The entire structure should later be reproduced in distinct semantic scenarios.

Candidate families:

1. scientific collaboration;
2. software engineering pair;
3. design team;
4. negotiation pair;
5. educational mentor–student dyad.

The primary pilot uses **scientific collaboration only**.

No cross-domain generalisation claim will be made from RH-1 alone.

---

# 23. Randomisation

For every experimental unit:

- branch ordering is randomised;
- role assignment of intervention receiver is randomised;
- lexical variants are sampled from a frozen template set;
- probe ordering is randomised for secondary probes only.

Primary probe position remains fixed.

---

# 24. Generation parameters

For the cleanest first experiment, use:

\[
T=0
\]

or the lowest deterministic generation mode practically available.

The first pilot tests state-history effects, not sampling variability.

A later robustness experiment introduces stochastic decoding.

---

# 25. Hidden-state extraction

Record, where technically available:

- residual stream;
- selected layer activations;
- attention-independent hidden representations;
- final-token and pooled turn representations.

The primary layer must be preregistered before outcome analysis.

A reasonable initial choice is a late-middle layer rather than the final output layer, because the latter may be excessively dominated by immediate lexical realisation.

Layer sweeps remain exploratory.

---

# 26. Trial exclusion

A paired trial is excluded if:

1. endpoint matching fails;
2. either agent breaks assigned role;
3. experimental manipulation is explicitly detected;
4. output truncates;
5. model refuses the benign task;
6. generation becomes non-deterministic because of infrastructure failure;
7. hidden-state extraction is incomplete;
8. one branch receives a different external task.

Exclusions are logged before probe analysis.

---

# 27. Pilot size

Generate:

\[
50
\]

paired units for each of the four arms:

\[
N,F,P^{-},P^{+}.
\]

Total:

\[
200
\]

paired experimental units.

Because each unit contains counterfactual branches, the actual number of generated trajectories will exceed this count.

The pilot is not confirmatory.

Its purposes are:

- estimate endpoint-matching success;
- estimate measurement variance;
- validate probes;
- identify ceiling/floor effects;
- determine confirmatory power.

---

# 28. Predefined failure modes

## Failure F1

The perturbation leaves large differences in \(A_T,B_T\).

Interpretation:

recovery protocol inadequate.

Not evidence for relational memory.

---

## Failure F2

Endpoints match but future behaviour does not differ.

Interpretation:

RH-1 receives no support.

---

## Failure F3

Future behaviour differs but the effect is also produced by factual control \(F\).

Interpretation:

general history effect rather than specifically relational history.

---

## Failure F4

Both \(P^{-}\) and \(P^{+}\) produce identical effects.

Interpretation:

social-event salience may explain the result better than relational valence.

---

## Failure F5

Path predicts behaviour only through residual endpoint differences.

Interpretation:

no evidence for an additional relational state.

---

# 29. Success criterion

The pilot becomes eligible for confirmatory replication if:

1. endpoint matching is feasible at acceptable frequency;
2. the positive manipulation affects the probe;
3. negative controls behave appropriately;
4. the relational perturbation retains non-negligible predictive information after endpoint adjustment;
5. the effect replicates across random seeds.

Only after these criteria are met will RH-1 be preregistered as a confirmatory experiment.

---

# 30. Interpretation boundary

A positive RH-1 permits the statement:

> **Interaction history contains persistent predictive information about future dyadic behaviour beyond terminal individual representations.**

A positive RH-1 does not yet permit:

> the latent state is octonionic;

> the relation contains zero divisors;

> Cayley–Dickson algebra is the uniquely correct explanation.

Those claims belong to RA-1, SE-1 and ZA-1.

---
